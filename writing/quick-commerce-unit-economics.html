What a SKU actually earns on Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart

I kept reading that quick commerce margins were thin, and I kept not finding anyone who showed the arithmetic. So I built it, line by line, from the filings and the rate cards that exist in public.

Preetam Priyabrat October 2026 12 min read All figures sourced below

Last year I was selling phone accessories. Silicone stands, cases, suction pads, nothing exotic. Somebody asked me why I was not on Blinkit yet, and I did not have a real answer. I had a vague sense that the commission was high and that was that. When I went looking for the actual numbers, what I found was a lot of confident writing and almost no arithmetic.

So I did the thing I usually end up doing, which is build the P&L myself and see what survives. This is that, with every number attributed, and with the gaps marked as gaps rather than filled in with something plausible.

Start here: Blinkit is not a marketplace any more

This is the fact that breaks every comparison written before late 2025, and a surprising number of people still have not updated for it.

From 1 September 2025, Blink Commerce Private Limited became the merchant of record. Blinkit now owns the inventory. You do not sell on Blinkit. You sell to Blinkit, which issues a purchase order and resells to the customer. By the January 2026 earnings call, Eternal's management said about 90% of the business had moved to the inventory model.1,2

The visible consequence is that Blinkit's reported revenue rose 552.7% year on year in Q1 FY27 without anything real changing, because gross merchandise value started flowing through the revenue line.3 Divide that revenue by net order value and you get roughly 91%, which some trackers have labelled a take rate. It is not. It is an accounting artefact. If you see 91% quoted as what Blinkit charges brands, the person quoting it has not read the filing.

The real consequence is that your negotiation changed shape. It is no longer a commission conversation. It is a buying margin conversation, the kind every FMCG company has had with modern trade for thirty years, except the buyer here runs 2,443 dark stores and 3 million orders a day.3 Expiry, shrinkage and pilferage moved onto Blinkit's books, which sounds like a gift until you realise they priced it into what they are willing to pay you. Blinkit reported inventory losses of ₹308 crore in a single quarter, about 1.8% of net order value, and the CFO described that number as fairly asymptotic.3 Somebody funds that, and it is not the customer.

What the platforms actually take

Here is where it gets uncomfortable, because the honest answer is that nobody publishes a rate card and the public estimates disagree by a factor of three.

Blinkit published a slab structure in March 2025, before the 1P shift: 2% under ₹500, 6% between ₹500 and ₹700, 18% above ₹1,200. Business Standard noted at the time that once storage, warehousing and delivery charges were added, the all-in take reached 30 to 35%.4

Category rate cards do not exist in public. The seller portals are behind JavaScript gates. What circulates instead comes from enablement agencies claiming to aggregate client terms, and I want to be clear that these are estimates. One such source puts beverages at 12 to 16%, snacks at 14 to 18%, personal care at 16 to 20%, and beauty at 18 to 22%, with commission charged on MRP rather than on the discounted price you actually realise.7 That last detail matters more than the percentage.

Separately, Inc42 has reported quick commerce commission at 35 to 50% of MRP.8 That is two to three times every other estimate I found. I think it is measuring all-in cost including advertising and discount funding rather than commission, but nobody says which, and that ambiguity is most of why this debate goes nowhere.

What I could not verify No platform publishes a fill rate penalty schedule. No primary source quantifies the split between brand-funded and platform-funded discounts. Nobody has published an audited brand-side quick commerce P&L in the Indian press. These are governed by bilateral agreements, and everything written about them is estimation.

The ad tax is the part people underestimate

Commission is the number everyone argues about. Advertising is the number that actually moves the P&L, and it is growing much faster.

Quick commerce advertising in India went from ₹1,325 crore in 2024 to ₹4,000 crore in 2025, with ₹6,000 crore projected for 2026.9 Zepto's own disclosure is the cleanest window into this: advertising revenue of ₹49 crore in FY24, ₹651 crore in FY25, ₹1,636 crore in FY26.10 In two years it became about 7% of their operating revenue. That money comes from brands.

On the brand side, Redseer puts beauty and personal care advertising at up to 10% of quick commerce sales.11 Named allocations reported by Exchange4media are starker: Sirona went from under 5% of total ad budget to around 20% in six months, Kidbea from 5 to 7% up to 15 to 20%, with brands collectively showing an eight to tenfold increase in quick commerce ad spend over 12 to 18 months while rates rose about 50%.12

Ad rates rose 40% in a year. Festival and cricket premiums roughly double them. Peak slot spending, the 7:30 to 9:30 morning and evening windows, has nearly doubled.13,14

Swiggy's own bridge to EBITDA breakeven is ₹13 to ₹14 per order, of which roughly ₹10 is expected to come from advertising. Rahul Bothra, CFO, on the Q1 FY27 earnings call.15 That is the ad tax stated as a business plan, by the people collecting it.

And then there is the measurement problem, which I think is the single most useful thing in this piece if you are running a quick commerce account right now. Platforms compute return on ad spend against MRP, not against what you actually realise after discount and commission. One agency analysis puts it plainly: a 7x ROAS on your Blinkit dashboard is about 4.7x on real transaction value.16 An independent benchmark set reaches the same place from the other direction, with platform-reported ROAS of 3.0 to 6.0x translating to true ROAS of 0.7 to 3.2x once cost of goods and commission come out.17

I have made this exact mistake in a smaller way. When I was running Meta ads for my own brand I spent a month optimising against a number that was measuring the wrong thing, and the account got better at producing the number while the bank balance did not move. It is a specific and quite humbling kind of failure.

Change advertising from 10% to 15%, which is where plenty of brands sit, and net contribution goes to roughly 3%. Change it to 20%, which is where a new brand buying its way onto a crowded shelf often lands, and you are paying for the privilege of selling.

That sensitivity is the whole thing. The commission is roughly fixed and roughly knowable. The ad line is the one you control, and it is the one that decides whether the channel makes money.

The basket is shrinking, and that is structural

Blinkit's net average order value went ₹521, then ₹525, then ₹518 across recent quarters. Management's guidance is that it is unlikely to rise even as assortment expands, and the reason they give is worth sitting with: as people order more often, they order fewer things each time. Older cohorts spend three times what they did three years ago, driven entirely by frequency rather than basket size.3

For the platform that is a good story. For a brand it is a slow squeeze, because your per-order advertising cost amortises across a smaller and smaller basket. The ROAS you modelled at ₹525 does not hold at ₹460.

So which one

If I were choosing today, these are the differences I would actually weigh.

Blinkit lets you hold price. It commands roughly a 15% premium over offline, 10% over Instamart and 5% over Zepto.18 For a brand whose positioning depends on not being the cheapest thing on the shelf, that is worth more than a two point commission difference. It also has the deepest pockets by a distance, ₹13,380 crore of net cash at the parent, and it is the only one of the three currently profitable.

Zepto moves the most product per store, 2,140 orders per store per day against Blinkit's 1,357 and Instamart's 1,093.19 It also has the thinnest balance sheet, with net cash of around ₹2,970 crore and an IPO still pending, alongside a CCI predatory pricing investigation and ED summons to its founders disclosed in the DRHP.20 Counterparty risk is a real line item when you are shipping them inventory on credit.

Instamart is the cheapest place to advertise and reaches the broadest set of cities, 1,171 stores across 131.5 It also has the most explicit private label strategy of the three. Swiggy's own clean-food brand plus its "Switch to Better" curation already account for more than 15% of category sales where they operate, and the CFO has described private brands as carrying significantly higher margins.15 Your ad spend is partly funding the merchandising engine of a competitor who can see your conversion data.

What I would do differently now

When I wrote a category thesis for Blinkit's Drinks and Juices shelf earlier this year, the thing that surprised me was how much of the margin problem was physical rather than commercial. Rider payload, cold chain slots, duplicate facings. The commercial terms were almost a distraction.

I think the same is true from the brand side. The questions that actually decide whether quick commerce works for you are not about the commission:

  1. What is your true ROAS, computed on net realisation rather than MRP? If you do not know, you do not know whether the channel is profitable.
  2. What happens to your contribution margin when AOV falls another 10%? Model it before it happens, because it is going to.
  3. Is this a profit channel or a discovery channel? Viraj Bahl at Veeba is unusually clear about this. Quick commerce is 8 to 10% of their revenue and general trade is over 70%, and his position is to earn from quick commerce and reinvest it into retail.21
  4. Can you hold price? If your brand cannot sustain a premium, Blinkit's main advantage is not available to you and the maths gets considerably harder.

The number I keep coming back to is Angshu Mallick's, the CEO of AWL Agri Business, in December 2025: the cost of doing business on quick commerce has risen to the point where margins are nearly the same as general trade.22 That is a remarkable sentence. The channel that was supposed to be a margin upgrade over kirana has converged with kirana, and it took about three years.

Which does not mean do not do it. It means do it with the arithmetic in front of you, and stop treating the dashboard ROAS as if it were true.
